# MATHCOUNTS Round formats, 2010–2025

Research for issue #10 (child of map #8). Vocabulary follows `GLOSSARY.md`: **Competition**, **Test**, **Round**, **Round format**.

Each claim carries a source tag. **[P]** marks a primary source (mathcounts.org pages and MATHCOUNTS-published PDFs). **[S]** marks a secondary source, used only where the primary sources are silent. Sources are listed at the end.

## Answer in one paragraph

The four written and oral Rounds kept the same Round format at every level from 2010 to 2025:

- **Sprint**: 30 problems, 40 minutes, no calculator, individual.
- **Target**: 8 problems in 4 pairs, 6 minutes per pair, calculator, individual.
- **Team**: 10 problems, 20 minutes, calculator, team of 4.
- **Countdown**: oral and head-to-head, at most 45 seconds per problem, no calculator, individual. The booklet holds about 80 problems.

The scoring formulas did not change either. What varies is:

1. **Which Rounds a Test contains.** The 2021 virtual Chapter, Chapter Invitational and State Tests had Sprint and Target only. The 2021 National Test had no Countdown. National Countdown problems are almost never published.
2. **Whether Countdown counts** (official, unofficial or omitted). This is an administration fact, not a Round format.

Competition-level defaults keyed by Round, plus Tests that contain only the Rounds they actually have, are enough. No per-level defaults are needed, and on current evidence no per-Test format override is ever exercised for 2010–2025. The one schema need is a time limit that can apply per Round, per group of problems (Target pairs) or per problem (Countdown).

## Round formats (unchanged 2010–2025)

| Round | Problems | Time limit | Calculator | Who | Administration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sprint | 30 | 40 min for the whole Round | No | Individual | One booklet. Tests accuracy; the time allows "only the most capable students to complete all of the problems" [P1][P2][P3]. |
| Target | 8 | 6 min per pair (about 30 min total) | Yes | Individual | Problems are handed out in 4 pairs of 2, and each pair is timed separately [P1][P2][P3]. |
| Team | 10 | 20 min | Yes | Team (up to 4) | Only the 4 registered team members take it officially. Teams of fewer than 4 may compete, but the score is still divided by 4 [P1][P2]. |
| Countdown | No fixed count in the rules. Booklets hold 80 problems (e.g. "2024 Chapter Competition Countdown Round Problems 1−80" [P6]). | At most 45 s per problem | No | Individual, head-to-head | Oral buzzer round for top scorers, described below [P2][P3]. |

Rules common to all Rounds [P1][P2]:

- The written Rounds are designed to take about 3 hours together, and the coordinator chooses their order.
- A Tiebreaker Round (calculator allowed, "if needed") is mentioned in the rules but never published as a Round [P1][P4].

### Countdown procedure (Chapter and State, when official) [P2]

- The top 25% of students by Individual Score, up to a maximum of 10, qualify.
- It runs as a ladder. The two lowest-ranked students meet first. Each question allows 45 s, and the first correct buzz scores. A pair gets 3 questions, and whoever has more points advances (ties continue until someone answers correctly). The winner challenges the next-higher-ranked student.
- In the final four matchups, the first student to answer 3 questions correctly advances.
- An official Countdown sets final individual rank. An unofficial one is "for practice or for fun", and individual winners then come from Sprint and Target scores alone.
- "Rules for the Countdown Round change for the National Competition" [P2].

### National Countdown

- It is official every year it is held, and it decides the National Champion [P1][P2].
- The top 12 written scorers qualify [P7].
- **[S]** It runs as a single-elimination bracket, with byes for the top 4 written scorers. Later matches are first to 3 correct [S1][S2]. No primary source states the match rules.

### Scoring (unchanged) [P1][P2][P4]

- **Individual Score** = Sprint correct + 2 × Target correct. The maximum is 46.
- **Team Score** = (sum of members' Individual Scores) ÷ 4 + 2 × Team correct. The maximum is 66.
- An official Countdown overrides written rank for final individual standings.
- Tiebreaks: for individuals, the higher Sprint score wins, then specific Sprint and Target question groups are compared. For teams, the higher Team Round score wins, then the higher sum of members' Sprint scores, then specific Team questions.

I found no scoring change in any primary source between 2014–15 [P2] and 2026–27 [P1].

## Changes and exceptions, 2010–2025

- **2010–2011 Masters Round (National only).** **[S]** This was an oral-presentation round at National that did not affect ranking. It was dropped from the 2012 National in favour of the Reel Math (later Math Video) Challenge [S1][S2]. It has no problem set in the source for 2010–2025 [S3], so it is not a Round we would import.
- **2020.** School, Chapter and State Tests were published with all 4 Rounds [P4][S3]. The National Competition was cancelled because of COVID-19 [S3].
- **2021 virtual season.** Everything ran online. There were "4 levels of official competition—chapter, chapter invitational, state and national—and 4 unofficial online practice competitions". "Team and Countdown Rounds will not be conducted officially … until the national level" [S4, quoting MATHCOUNTS].
  - MATHCOUNTS' own list of what was published says [P4]:
    - "2021 Chapter Invitational & State | Sprint & Target Rounds"
    - "2021 National | Sprint, Target & Team Rounds"
  - The 2021 Chapter Test likewise has only Sprint and Target [S3].
  - There was no 2021 National Countdown. "The National Champion is determined during an individual, proctored written exam at the online 2021 … National Competition" (mathcounts.org How It Works page, as quoted in search results; the page is now behind a login).
  - Sprint stayed at 30 problems and 40 minutes online: the 2021 Chapter Invitational Sprint cover reads "This section of the competition consists of 30 problems. You will have 40 minutes" [P4].
- **Countdown official, optional or unofficial.**
  - Chapter and State: the state's choice throughout [P1][P2].
  - School: optional [P3].
  - National: always official when held.
  - 2021: not run officially at any level [P4][S4].
- **Practice materials in older years: Warm-Ups and Workouts.**
  - The School Handbook practice problems are organised as Warm-Ups (10 problems each, no calculator, practice for Sprint and Countdown), Workouts (10 problems each, calculator, practice for Target and Team) and Stretches (topic sets, mixed calculator use).
  - The counts vary by year: 18 Warm-Ups and 9 Workouts in 2014–15 [P2]; 14 and 8 in 2018–19 [P3] and 2021–22 [P4]; 11 and 6 in 2023–24 [P8].
  - These are handbook practice sets, not competition Rounds. The source site files "Warmups" and "Workouts" only under 2005–2007 practice sets, so none fall inside 2010–2025 [S3].
- **Advancement detail (not a format).** A school may send 1 team of 4 plus individuals. That was "up to six" individuals in 2014–15 [P2] and "10 individuals" in the 2026–27 wording [P1]. This does not affect Round format.

## Conclusion for the model

1. **Problem count, time limit, calculator and individual/team are constant per Round across every level and year, 2010–2025.** One default Round format per Round at the MATHCOUNTS Competition level covers every Test. Per-level defaults are not needed, which fits the decision not to model levels. No primary source shows a Test that needs a format override. Keep the override mechanism for safety, since the 2021 online Tests ran under unusual conditions, but expect it to be empty.
2. **The real variation is which Rounds exist in a Test.** Examples:
   - 2021 Chapter, Chapter Invitational and State have Sprint and Target only.
   - 2021 National has Sprint, Target and Team.
   - National Tests are usually missing Countdown because it is unpublished.
   - School Tests after 2012 are missing Countdown in the source.

   This needs no new concept. A Test simply has the Rounds it has, and the Competition's default Round formats are a catalogue that a Test's Rounds draw on. The model must not assume every MATHCOUNTS Test has all 4 Rounds.
3. **Round format needs more than one flat time limit.**
   - Target is timed per pair: 4 groups of 2 problems, 6 minutes each.
   - Countdown is timed per problem (45 s) and has no fixed problem count (booklets have 80, and a match uses only some of them).

   Suggested fields:
   - problem count (nullable for Countdown)
   - time limit, with a scope of round, group or problem
   - group size (2 for Target)
   - calculator (yes/no)
   - individual or team
   - optionally, oral/head-to-head, to mark Countdown
4. **Leave out of Round format:**
   - whether Countdown was official at a given Test (an administration fact that varies by state and year, and is irrelevant to studying the problems)
   - scoring weights (Target ×2, Team ×2), which are constant and out of scope unless scoring is ever simulated
   - the Tiebreaker and Masters Rounds, which have no published problem sets in range

## Gaps

- I found no publicly reachable School Handbook for 2010–11 through 2013–14, so the rules text for 2010–2013 is not directly cited. The 2012 National Sprint booklet and the source site's Round lists match the later rules, and secondary sources describe no change.
- The National Countdown match rules are not in any primary source I could reach. MATHCOUNTS gives competition officials "more detailed procedures" that are not public [P2].
- The mathcounts.org 2020–21 "How It Works" page is now behind a login, so the 2020–21 wording comes from search excerpts and a school flyer that quotes it.
